Origin

It started with a problem we knew well.

In early 2026, I started a company with a small group of fellow UC Berkeley Division 1 athletes. The idea came directly out of our own experience: we had spent years dealing with the problem we are now working on, and we felt we understood it well enough to build something better.

Before writing any code, we spent over a month interviewing athletes, coaches, and program staff to test our assumptions. That research still drives most of our product decisions.

The specifics stay quiet for now, but the product is in the hands of real users, and we learn from them every week.

How we work

Four habits that keep us moving.

None of these are complicated on their own. Together, they are a big part of how a team this small keeps shipping.

  1. Talk to users first

    Every feature traces back to something an athlete, coach, or staff member told us in an interview or asked for after using the product.

  2. Stay close to the problem

    We prioritize what our users report over what looks impressive, even when the fix is unglamorous.

  3. Use AI heavily

    AI tooling handles a large share of our engineering work, which is how a founding team without a full engineering staff ships quickly.

  4. Ship to a small group

    We keep a tight feedback loop with a limited set of users, and expand only when what we learn tells us to.

A month of interviews came before the first line of code.

Where AI fits

AI is central to how we build.

A team our size can only ship at this pace because AI carries a large share of the engineering work. It shapes how we architect the product, how fast we prototype, and how quickly user feedback becomes a released change.

We also lean on it beyond code: synthesizing interview notes, drafting communication, and thinking through product decisions.

Where it shows up

  • Product architecture and feature development
  • Rapid prototyping and iteration speed
  • Synthesizing user research and interviews
  • Day-to-day written communication

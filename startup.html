Origin

A problem we lived before we tried to solve it.

In early 2026, I teamed up with a small group of fellow UC Berkeley Division 1 athletes to start a company. None of us came at the idea from the outside. We had spent years inside the exact problem we are now trying to fix, which is part of why we felt we had something worth building.

Before a line of code was written, we spent over a month just listening: interviewing athletes, coaches, and program staff, and pressure-testing every assumption we had. That insider perspective still shapes every decision we make, from what we build to how we talk about it.

We are keeping specifics quiet for now. What I can say is that the product is already in the hands of real users, and we are learning from that every week.

How we work

Four habits we don’t skip.

None of these are groundbreaking on their own. Together, they are a big part of why a team this small can move this fast.

  1. Listen before building

    Every feature starts as something a real person told us they needed, not something that sounded good in a planning document.

  2. Chase the problem, not the trend

    We would rather solve something real in a plain way than chase whatever technology looks impressive this quarter.

  3. Let AI carry the weight

    From architecture to iteration, AI is how a founding team without a large engineering staff still ships on pace with much bigger teams.

  4. Stay small on purpose

    Instead of a broad launch, we are staying in tight feedback loops with a small group of real users before we scale up.

Before we ever asked someone to use what we built, we made sure we had earned that ask.

Where AI fits

Built AI-native, not AI-decorated.

AI is not a feature we bolted on after the fact, it is the reason a team our size can build at all. It shows up in how we architect the product, how fast we can prototype, and how quickly user feedback turns into a shipped change.

That said, it does not stop at code. It also shapes how we read through user research, how we draft communication, and how we think through product decisions.

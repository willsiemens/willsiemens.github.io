Dealer Bot

ESP32 Stepper motors Servo control SolidWorks Gear transmission Belt drive

A fully assembled automated card-dealing robot built to shuffle, organize, and dispense playing cards with repeatable feeding, controlled trajectory, and reliable player-to-player distribution.

Project objective

System goals

This project integrates a moving mechanical assembly with microcontroller-driven logic. The main goal was to create a machine that could combine the common functions of shuffling and dealing into one compact system.

The core engineering challenge was to separate a single card from a stack, move it into a controlled eject position, and direct it toward the correct player location. The finished build combines mechanical design, rapid prototyping, motor control, transmission design, web-based interaction, and iterative testing.

Subsystems

Mechanical and controls architecture

Mechanical dispensing

Engineered a friction-based roller and feed concept to separate and deal single cards from a standard deck while reducing jams. The chassis and brackets were modeled in SolidWorks and 3D printed.

Transmission systems

Used gear trains and belt drive systems as the main mechanical transmission methods to transfer motor output into controlled hopper movement, pusher motion, card feeding, and rotational positioning.

Motion control

Used stepper motors, servo mechanisms, and controlled DC motor output to manage hopper motion, pusher motion, base rotation, and card ejection.

Embedded logic

Built microcontroller logic for commands, homing routines, calibration, game flow, and repeatable card-dealing sequences across multiple player positions.

Calibration workflow

Designed the control logic around repeatable setup routines so player positions, card ejection behavior, and mechanical limits could be adjusted and validated during testing.

System integration

Refined the full machine through repeated mechanical testing, debugging, and design updates to improve feeding reliability, reduce missed motion events, and complete the final demonstration.

Build constraints

Key engineering challenges

  • Separating one card at a time without double-feeding.
  • Maintaining repeatable motion across different deal distances.
  • Coordinating gear, belt, and roller-driven motion with software timing.
  • Matching mechanical tolerances with consistent electronic control.
  • Designing a control system that is easy to calibrate, test, and demonstrate.

Final outcomes

The completed project demonstrates a functional automated dealer bot that can organize cards, execute controlled dealing motions, and support smoother game flow through embedded control.

The final system successfully combines mechanical card handling, gear and belt transmission, motor-driven actuation, calibration logic, and user interaction into one working mechatronics platform.

The project became a strong test bed for mechanical iteration, embedded controls, transmission design, and practical debugging under real hardware constraints.
